# Simple Light Mode

## Role and communication job

Use this style when the brief matches **general business presentation, clean light presentation, and research briefing**. It is designed for
general, internal, and external audiences. A clear reading surface: the deck feels calm because each page decides what deserves attention. The deck should help the audience make the
change implied by the work, not merely browse attractive pages. Its natural
story arc is **question → evidence → interpretation → recommendation**. It is not a good fit for **dense operating scorecard or dark visual direction**; choose
another style or record the mismatch instead of forcing this grammar.

## Visual grammar

### Surface, palette, and type

- Use warm or neutral paper, dark ink, and one selective accent. Long rules and open edges can organize the page without boxes.
- Use conclusion titles, readable body text, and small but present source notes. Keep a predictable scale between title, evidence, and caption; never solve overflow by silent shrinking.
- Keep units, dates, source basis, and uncertainty adjacent to the evidence
  they qualify. A visual hierarchy must not erase the conditions behind a claim.

### Geometry, layers, and motif

- Let an argument occupy a generous field: a chart, photo, comparison line, or typographic statement. Use unequal margins and rules to separate decisions.
- Use native Office text, charts, tables, connectors, SVG, and images when they
  carry the page's claim. Keep important foreground content editable.
- For a full-page image, use the real scene stack: background image → tested
  scrim or contrast field → editable claim/evidence → foreground markers. Keep
  axes, labels, connectors, decision lines, and chart marks unobstructed. If the
  stack cannot be proven after rendering, use a bounded image region or refuse
  the treatment.

## Page composition

### Preferred page archetypes

Use these archetypes as a vocabulary, not fixed layouts:

1. **question opener** — define the audience task, scope, and one visual
   signal before adding supporting material.
2. **evidence spread** — give one dominant carrier the space needed to be
   understood, with the shortest useful explanation.
3. **comparison or method** — expose the relationship, method, or comparison with
   lines, axes, labels, or a measured table.
4. **verdict close** — state the implication, owner, condition, or next
   move; do not end on an ornamental summary.

### Information carriers

- Favor one meaningful chart, photo, comparison line, table, or typographic statement per page. Use direct labels and captions rather than ornamental icons.
- Choose one meaningful image rather than a gallery. Full-bleed fields are welcome when crop, contrast, and text placement are deliberate.
- A chart, table, photograph, SVG, or geometric composition is valid only when
  it communicates scale, sequence, comparison, context, or identity. Remove
  decorative shapes that do not support the page's conclusion.
- Prefer direct labels and short reading cues over unexplained legends or icons.
  If a framework has no meaningful relationship to show, use typography and a
  rule instead of manufacturing a diagram.

## Density and rhythm

The expected density is **sparse to medium: use open pages as pauses and concentrate detail around the evidence that earns attention.**. Use whitespace to separate decisions, not to leave every page equally empty. Alternate a quiet statement with a denser proof page. Do not distribute
whitespace evenly by habit; concentrate it around the title-to-body transition,
carrier edges, column gutters, and final implication. If the brief calls for a
sparse page, record what the empty field is doing.

## Style-specific playbook

- **Page skeleton.** State the question, give the reader a dominant evidence field, explain the implication, and finish with a clear verdict or next question.
- **Type rhythm.** Keep the title, evidence, caption, unit, and source roles
  distinct. Let the largest type carry the reading decision, not the ornament.
- **Evidence carriers.** Favor one meaningful chart, photo, comparison line, table, or typographic statement per page. Use direct labels and captions rather than ornamental icons.
- **Images and layers.** Choose one meaningful image rather than a gallery. Full-bleed fields are welcome when crop, contrast, and text placement are deliberate. Inspect crop, contrast, and z-order after
  rendering; do not call an obscured line or label intentional without evidence.
- **Pacing.** Use whitespace to separate decisions, not to leave every page equally empty. Alternate a quiet statement with a denser proof page. Let adjacent pages change energy or silhouette when
  the narrative changes, while the design grammar remains recognizable.
- **Selection rule.** Choose this style when readability and editorial calm matter more than a dramatic stage effect.

## Signature moves and variation limits

- Repeat the title, source, and rule treatment while changing the evidence carrier.
- Use the accent sparingly to point at the active comparison or conclusion.
- Keep open space purposeful: focus, separation, pacing, or a declared sparse moment.
- If a user supplies a brand system or reference authority, it wins. This guide
  fills undefined choices and must not be blended with a conflicting style.

## Anti-patterns and refusal boundaries

- Avoid template-like title boxes, uniform cards, tiny captions, and decorative geometry without a reading job.
- Do not use a pale field to hide weak hierarchy or missing source context.
- Do not let background fields cover editable text, chart marks, or labels.
- Do not invent facts, sources, or visual meaning. Mark unresolved design facts
  as unresolved and ask only when the conflict changes the communication result.
- If a requested effect would hide evidence, alter an unknown native object, or
  require a fixed page clone, keep the safe portions and state the boundary.

## Calibration evidence

Inspect all four example images before composing. The four examples demonstrate a question, evidence, comparison, and verdict page; inspect them for reading rhythm rather than copying geometry. Recompose
every real page from its communication task. Never reuse their wording,
geometry, image bytes, or implied source deck.

## Review checklist

- The page has one audience task, one conclusion, and one primary visual carrier.
- Check title read-through, source/basis treatment, units, labels, and whether
  the final implication follows from the evidence.
- Text, image crop, contrast field, chart marks, connectors, and foreground
  labels survive a rendered visual check without occlusion or overflow.
- The deck varies page silhouette and density with purpose instead of repeating
  a wall of equal modules.
- Reopen the exported PPTX and structurally inspect it. Record the absolute
  path, SHA-256, and visual-review state before delivery.
