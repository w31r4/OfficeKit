# Scenario: business proposal

## Audience task

Help a sponsor understand a valuable opportunity, believe the proposed path is
credible, and approve a concrete commitment.

## Narrative

Move from the audience's situation and stakes to the proposed change, proof,
economics, delivery plan, risks, and a precise ask. Demonstrate value before
describing the team or feature inventory.

## Density and rhythm

Open with a small number of memorable claims. Place denser proof and economics
in the middle. Return to a sparse, explicit decision at the close. Keep the
reader version self-sufficient through notes or appendix evidence.

## Visual carriers and archetypes

- before/after operating model;
- value-chain or outcome map;
- proof page built around one customer or measured result;
- unit economics or investment-return chart;
- phased delivery path with owners and gates;
- final ask with scope, timing, and next action.

## Visual grammar

Use confident scale contrast, a clear directional motif, and surfaces that
separate promise from proof. Rectangles may frame commitments or phases; avoid
turning every sentence into a card. Imagery should show the product, customer,
or operating reality rather than generic aspiration.

## Avoid

Slogan stacks, feature catalogs, invented testimonials, oversized market numbers
without method, vague asks, decorative funnels, and a closing page that hides
commercial or delivery conditions.

## Review questions

- Is the proposal framed in the audience's terms rather than the seller's?
- Is every important promise paired with evidence or a stated assumption?
- Are economics, risks, responsibilities, and the ask easy to find?
- Would the deck still support a decision when read without the presenter?
