# Market Trends Report

## Role and communication job

Use this style when the brief matches **market trends report, industry outlook, competitive landscape, and investment thesis**. It is designed for
executives, clients, strategy teams, and investors. A dark signal atlas: the deck moves from a market contour to the few forces that change the decision. The deck should help the audience make the
change implied by the work, not merely browse attractive pages. Its natural
story arc is **market contour → force map → evidence → scenarios → stance**. It is not a good fit for **internal operating scorecard or print-first handout**; choose
another style or record the mismatch instead of forcing this grammar.

## Visual grammar

### Surface, palette, and type

- Use a deep neutral field, one luminous signal, and quiet secondary tones. Reserve the brightest value for the active signal; let history and uncertainty recede without disappearing.
- Use short declarative titles and generous numeric scale. Keep axis labels, source basis, and caveats quieter but readable.
- Keep units, dates, source basis, and uncertainty adjacent to the evidence
  they qualify. A visual hierarchy must not erase the conditions behind a claim.

### Geometry, layers, and motif

- Use wide trend fields, maps, annotated trajectories, and strong directional contours. Dark surfaces may hold a chapter or stance; evidence remains legible and grounded.
- Use native Office text, charts, tables, connectors, SVG, and images when they
  carry the page's claim. Keep important foreground content editable.
- For a full-page image, use the real scene stack: background image → tested
  scrim or contrast field → editable claim/evidence → foreground markers. Keep
  axes, labels, connectors, decision lines, and chart marks unobstructed. If the
  stack cannot be proven after rendering, use a bounded image region or refuse
  the treatment.

## Page composition

### Preferred page archetypes

Use these archetypes as a vocabulary, not fixed layouts:

1. **market opening** — define the audience task, scope, and one visual
   signal before adding supporting material.
2. **force map** — give one dominant carrier the space needed to be
   understood, with the shortest useful explanation.
3. **trend field** — expose the relationship, method, or comparison with
   lines, axes, labels, or a measured table.
4. **scenario decision** — state the implication, owner, condition, or next
   move; do not end on an ornamental summary.

### Information carriers

- Favor trend lines, indexed trajectories, market maps, scenario bands, and one decisive takeaway. Direct-label evidence instead of hiding meaning in legends.
- Use a full-bleed photograph, satellite texture, or restrained SVG only as context for a market force. Put a tested overlay between the image and editable text.
- A chart, table, photograph, SVG, or geometric composition is valid only when
  it communicates scale, sequence, comparison, context, or identity. Remove
  decorative shapes that do not support the page's conclusion.
- Prefer direct labels and short reading cues over unexplained legends or icons.
  If a framework has no meaningful relationship to show, use typography and a
  rule instead of manufacturing a diagram.

## Density and rhythm

The expected density is **medium: alternate expansive signal pages with focused analytical pages; avoid both an empty gallery and an unreadable dashboard.**. Move from broad contour to explanatory forces to scenario choice. Use a short transition only when the market frame truly changes. Do not distribute
whitespace evenly by habit; concentrate it around the title-to-body transition,
carrier edges, column gutters, and final implication. If the brief calls for a
sparse page, record what the empty field is doing.

## Style-specific playbook

- **Page skeleton.** Open with the market question and an orientation signal. Give the central field to a contour or map, then add the few annotated forces that change the forecast.
- **Type rhythm.** Keep the title, evidence, caption, unit, and source roles
  distinct. Let the largest type carry the reading decision, not the ornament.
- **Evidence carriers.** Favor trend lines, indexed trajectories, market maps, scenario bands, and one decisive takeaway. Direct-label evidence instead of hiding meaning in legends.
- **Images and layers.** Use a full-bleed photograph, satellite texture, or restrained SVG only as context for a market force. Put a tested overlay between the image and editable text. Inspect crop, contrast, and z-order after
  rendering; do not call an obscured line or label intentional without evidence.
- **Pacing.** Move from broad contour to explanatory forces to scenario choice. Use a short transition only when the market frame truly changes. Let adjacent pages change energy or silhouette when
  the narrative changes, while the design grammar remains recognizable.
- **Selection rule.** Choose this style when an audience must interpret external signals and decide what to watch, fund, or change.

## Signature moves and variation limits

- Use one active signal per page and let secondary series remain quiet.
- Repeat coordinate cues and annotation style, not a fixed panel arrangement.
- Let uncertainty show as a band, range, or alternate path rather than false precision.
- If a user supplies a brand system or reference authority, it wins. This guide
  fills undefined choices and must not be blended with a conflicting style.

## Anti-patterns and refusal boundaries

- Avoid neon gradients everywhere, floating pills, decorative circles, and three-column card dashboards.
- Do not imply a forecast without period, basis, and uncertainty.
- Do not let a contour, line, or label disappear behind a foreground block.
- Do not invent facts, sources, or visual meaning. Mark unresolved design facts
  as unresolved and ask only when the conflict changes the communication result.
- If a requested effect would hide evidence, alter an unknown native object, or
  require a fixed page clone, keep the safe portions and state the boundary.

## Calibration evidence

Inspect all four example images before composing. The four examples demonstrate a dark market opening, force analysis, data field, and scenario close; they calibrate signal hierarchy, not page geometry. Recompose
every real page from its communication task. Never reuse their wording,
geometry, image bytes, or implied source deck.

## Review checklist

- The page has one audience task, one conclusion, and one primary visual carrier.
- Check title read-through, source/basis treatment, units, labels, and whether
  the final implication follows from the evidence.
- Text, image crop, contrast field, chart marks, connectors, and foreground
  labels survive a rendered visual check without occlusion or overflow.
- The deck varies page silhouette and density with purpose instead of repeating
  a wall of equal modules.
- Reopen the exported PPTX and structurally inspect it. Record the absolute
  path, SHA-256, and visual-review state before delivery.
