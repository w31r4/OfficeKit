# Scenario: management report

## Audience task

Help leaders understand what changed, why it changed, what needs attention, who
owns the response, and what decision or alignment is required. Lock reporting
periods, baselines, thresholds, status definitions, owners, and dates.

The deck is a management instrument, not an activity archive. Material variance
and action receive more attention than stable metrics.

## Narrative

Lead with the operating headline, then move through material outcomes, drivers,
exceptions, interventions, owners, and forward risks. Organize around decisions
and variance rather than the reporting org chart. Separate observed result,
diagnosed cause, planned response, and requested escalation.

## Density and rhythm

Use an executive opening followed by evidence pages of controlled density.
Alternate performance views with driver or action pages. Reserve sparse pages
for decisions, escalations, and section turns. Keep repeated metric views
visually stable while varying the rest of the composition.

## Relationship-to-carrier choices

- operating headline with a compact, meaningful score strip;
- actual-versus-plan trend with annotated inflections;
- driver decomposition or contribution chart;
- exception table with threshold, owner, due date, and state;
- operating flow or dependency map for a blocked outcome;
- forward outlook with triggers and accountable actions;
- decision page separating what management must approve from what the team owns.

Charts should show variance, trend, or contribution. Tables should be reserved
for exact exceptions and action ownership. Use screenshots or operational images
only when they explain the constraint better than a diagram or metric.

## Visual grammar

Use disciplined alignment, stable metric roles, tabular rhythm, thin rules, and
rectangular regions with clear hierarchy. Distinguish status, variance, risk,
and action; do not reuse one accent for all four. Use restrained rounding only
where a grouped surface improves scanning.

Typography should make the operating headline and exception states immediately
visible. Sources and time periods remain close to the numbers. Use lines to
connect drivers to outcomes or owners to actions, not as ornamental separators.

## Avoid

KPI card walls, traffic-light color without thresholds, tiny footnotes,
repeated three-column templates, activity reports without outcomes, ownerless
action lists, and executive summaries that merely repeat every page title.

## Review questions

- Can a leader identify the material change and required action in one minute?
- Are drivers separated from symptoms and activities?
- Do exceptions lead to an owner, date, threshold, or decision?
- Can the deck serve as a durable record after the meeting?
- If a page feels like a dashboard, which decision relationship should replace
  the inventory before styling is changed?
