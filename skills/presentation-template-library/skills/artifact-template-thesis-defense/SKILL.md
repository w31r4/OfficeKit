# Thesis Defense

## Communication territory

A formal defense narrative that prioritizes the research contribution while preserving enough evidence for challenge.

Use this style for doctoral defense, research proposal defense, academic committee briefing. Avoid it for sales pitch, casual workshop.

This is a visual direction, not a page library. Read the calibration images for hierarchy, rhythm, and relationships. Build every slide from the current content and do not trace sample geometry or reuse sample wording.

## Design grammar

- **Composition:** Use a clear progression from gap to method to result to contribution, with one anchor figure per substantive page.
- **Typography:** Authoritative serif headlines and calm sans-serif explanation create formality without looking ceremonial.
- **Geometry and surface:** Use clean axes, citation rails, restrained brackets, and deliberate chapter markers.
- **Evidence:** Charts and tables must be readable at presentation distance; defer secondary detail to notes or appendix.
- **Imagery:** Use fieldwork or artifact images only when they are part of the evidence, not to humanize an abstract topic.
- **Recurring motif:** A violet contribution marker can recur at each point where the work advances prior knowledge.
- **Density:** Use a medium default, then vary it intentionally between orientation, proof, and decision pages.
- **Palette roles:** Treat #FBFAF7 as the base field, #172A3A as primary ink, #7A4EA3 as the decisive accent, #C18A2D as supporting evidence, and #BBC4CC as quiet structure. Adapt values when a user design system is authoritative.

## Page behavior

- Give every page one audience task, one claim, and one primary visual carrier.
- Derive new page silhouettes from this grammar. Do not repeat one arrangement across the deck.
- Keep charts, diagrams, images, and typography in one information relationship; do not place them as independent decorations.
- Use native editable PowerPoint objects and licensed or user-provided imagery. Render and review every page.

## Avoid

- Literature review walls.
- Unreadable regression tables.
- Graduation motifs.
- Do not mix another template's visual language. A user-provided design system or reference deck remains authoritative.
- Do not copy the calibration text or coordinates; they demonstrate behavior, not reusable pages.

## Calibration examples

- **Cover:** establishes the voice, scale contrast, and opening visual contract.
- **Analysis:** shows how a claim and its supporting relationship share the canvas.
- **Data:** shows how evidence receives visual priority and direct annotation.
- **Closing:** shows how the direction compresses into a specific decision or next move.
