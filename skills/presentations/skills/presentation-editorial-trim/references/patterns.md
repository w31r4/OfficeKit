# Presentation copy patterns

Use this reference after facts are locked. These are diagnostic questions for
audience-facing slides, not a mechanical replacement list.

## Copy layers and evidence locks

Preserve exact names, values, dates, units, citations, quotations, uncertainty,
technical identifiers, legal wording, and user-approved phrases. A page may
change from verbose to concise while every protected token and the causal
meaning around it stays intact.

Edit each layer for its own job:

| Layer | Good test | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Title or claim | Can the audience tell why this page matters? | Topic label, slogan, or production note |
| Visible support | Does it explain the evidence the visual cannot? | Paragraph that repeats the title and chart |
| Label or source | Can the evidence be interpreted and traced? | Clever paraphrase, missing unit, detached citation |
| Speaker notes | Does it help delivery without carrying essential visible logic? | Hidden conclusion or a verbatim slide transcript |

## Patterns worth challenging

Challenge a pattern only when it is not required by evidence, genre, or voice.

- **False contrast:** “This is not X; it is Y” when Y can be stated directly.
- **Defensive negation:** several sentences explaining what the page does not
  claim before saying what it does claim.
- **Throat clearing:** “It is worth noting,” “As we can see,” or a paragraph of
  setup before the evidence.
- **Report signposts:** “First, second, finally” used where the visual sequence
  already communicates order.
- **Abstract noun chains:** strings such as “capability construction and value
  realization” without a concrete actor or action.
- **Repeated triads:** every page using three parallel nouns, cards, or clauses
  whether the evidence has three parts or not.
- **Unsupported superlatives:** “revolutionary,” “unprecedented,” “industry-
  leading,” or “全面领先” without a source and comparison basis.
- **Continuous metaphor:** one metaphor stretched across pages after it stops
  clarifying the subject.
- **Slogan fragments:** clipped phrases whose energy comes from punctuation
  rather than meaning.
- **Repeated title openings:** consecutive pages beginning with the same word,
  syntax, or “从…到…” construction.

## Better transformations

Prefer a concrete subject, verb, evidence, and consequence:

```text
Before: 不是流量红利，而是组织能力的系统性跃迁
After: 续费提升主要来自交付周期缩短，而非新增流量

Before: 三大引擎，全面驱动增长新范式
After: 渠道扩张贡献了本季度新增收入的 62%

Before: It is important to note that latency has improved significantly.
After: P95 latency fell from 840 ms to 310 ms after queue isolation.
```

The replacement is valid only when its fact is present in the source. When the
source cannot support specificity, narrow the claim or state the uncertainty;
do not invent a number to improve the sentence.

## Title spectrum

Use the form that matches the page job:

- claim: “ETF inflows absorbed eight days of new supply”;
- decision: “Approve the staged migration with a 10% traffic gate”;
- question: “Which constraint now limits recovery time?”;
- instruction: “Verify the rollback signal before expanding traffic”;
- section turn: a short phrase when the next sequence needs a pause.

Topic labels are useful for navigation and reference decks, but they should not
silently replace a conclusion the audience needs.

## Page-fit repair order

When copy does not fit:

1. remove duplicated interpretation;
2. move presenter-only detail to notes when allowed;
3. shorten labels without changing terminology;
4. split an overloaded page or change its carrier;
5. adjust composition and spacing;
6. reduce type only within the declared readability floor.

Do not delete evidence qualifiers, citations, or source labels to rescue fit.
