# Scenario: academic research

## Audience task

Help an audience evaluate a question, method, evidence, uncertainty, and
contribution without overstating the result. Lock sample, method, variables,
notation, confidence language, citations, and limitations before editing.

The deck may explain, defend, or invite critique; record which of these is the
primary job because it changes the order and density of evidence.

## Narrative

Establish the question and prior gap, explain method and data, present results,
test alternatives or limitations, interpret cautiously, and state the
contribution and next inquiry. Put the information required to interpret a
result before the result, not in a later appendix.

## Density and rhythm

Use deliberate progression and consistent analytical views. Give methods and
uncertainty enough room to be inspectable. Insert sparse synthesis pages between
dense evidence sequences. A live defense may reveal a figure in stages, but
the exported page must still make sense without playback.

## Relationship-to-carrier choices

- research question and conceptual model;
- sample, method, or study-flow diagram;
- annotated figure with comparable scales and visible units;
- result with uncertainty interval or distribution;
- robustness, alternative explanation, or limitation comparison;
- contribution and open-questions synthesis;
- appendix evidence with stable locators from the main narrative.

Prefer native charts or legible source figures over decorative science imagery.
Tables serve exact coefficients or method parameters; diagrams serve process,
causal assumptions, or model structure. Keep citations adjacent to the claim.

## Visual grammar

Use stable notation, quiet surfaces, precise lines, figure-first compositions,
and highly legible labels. Color encodes variables, groups, or confidence
consistently. Geometry should clarify study stages and comparison sets rather
than mimic a generic research poster.

Typography must preserve symbols and terminology. Use spacing to separate
observation from interpretation. Motifs may identify method, result, or
limitation roles, but decoration never competes with evidence.

## Avoid

Decorative science imagery, causal language for exploratory evidence, hidden
denominators, inconsistent notation, unreadable figure imports, oversized
conclusions, detached references, and small multiples with incomparable axes.

## Review questions

- Can the audience distinguish question, method, result, and interpretation?
- Are uncertainty, limitation, and provenance visible at the point of use?
- Do comparable figures share notation, units, and scales?
- Does the conclusion stay within what the evidence supports?
- If comprehension fails, should notation, evidence order, or the figure itself
  be repaired before visual styling?
