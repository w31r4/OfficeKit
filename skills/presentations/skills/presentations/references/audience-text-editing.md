# Audience-facing text editing

Edit presentation text in four passes. Keep these passes separate so evidence
does not drift while prose becomes shorter.

## 1. Lock facts and sources

Identify measured values, names, dates, quotations, citations, assumptions,
and user-approved wording. Record them in the plan. Resolve contradictions
before compressing copy.

## 2. Rewrite for the audience

Write the conclusion the audience needs, the evidence that supports it, and
the action or interpretation that follows. Prefer concrete subjects and verbs.
State uncertainty where the evidence has it.

Titles should usually carry a claim or reader task. Labels identify objects;
body copy explains only what the visual cannot communicate by itself.

## 3. Compress against the real page

Render or inspect the composed page before shortening. Remove repetition,
empty transitions, duplicated labels, throat-clearing, and unsupported
adjectives. Preserve facts and logical qualifiers.

Do not solve overflow by shrinking all text. Change hierarchy, split evidence,
or choose a different composition when the page job is too large.

## 4. Review the deck voice

Read titles in sequence, then evidence captions and calls to action. Check:

- consistent level of certainty;
- stable terms for the same concept;
- natural variation in title syntax;
- no repeated slogans or mechanical three-part phrasing;
- no local rewrite outside the requested pages;
- every claim has visible support or an explicit assumption.

Global tone changes require explicit deck-wide scope. A local edit inherits the
existing voice and changes only the declared pages.
