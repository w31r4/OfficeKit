# Scenario: education and training

## Audience task

Help learners build a mental model, practice a procedure, or transfer a skill
they can use after the session.

## Narrative

Start from prior knowledge and the learning outcome. Sequence concept, example,
guided practice, feedback, application, and recap. Reveal complexity only after
the foundation is stable.

## Density and rhythm

Keep teaching pages focused and alternate explanation with demonstration or
practice. Use repetition for retrieval, not for visual cloning. Put detailed
reference material in handout-friendly pages or an appendix.

## Visual carriers and archetypes

- concept map;
- worked example with progressive annotation;
- before/after or correct/incorrect comparison;
- numbered procedure with visible state changes;
- practice prompt followed by answer rationale;
- recap map and transfer checklist.

## Visual grammar

Use consistent cues for definition, example, warning, and action. Connectors and
numbering must reveal sequence. Favor diagrams, highlighted excerpts, and
annotated screenshots over paragraphs inside decorative cards. Motion may stage
a process in live delivery but the static page must remain understandable.

## Avoid

Lecture transcripts on slides, unexplained jargon, quiz answers visible too
early, arbitrary clip art, tiny multi-step screenshots, and recaps that merely
repeat section titles.

## Review questions

- Is the learner outcome observable and practiced?
- Does each page build on knowledge already introduced?
- Are examples representative, legible, and explained?
- Can the deck function as a useful handout after the session?
