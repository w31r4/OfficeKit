# Project Kickoff

## Communication territory

Use this direction to align a team around why a project exists, what is in
scope, how work will proceed, and which risks need an owner before execution.
It serves mixed technical and business audiences. It is not a status report
and it should not turn the plan into a list of slogans.

## Visual grammar

Begin with a confident dark green or ink field and one bright lime or sky
signal for the promise of the project. Interior pages can use a quiet warm
field, dark type, and the same signal as a route through the deck. A small
capsule is allowed for a genuine phase or date label, but ordinary hierarchy
comes from type, alignment, and rules. Keep titles declarative and put the
time horizon beside the claim.

## Page vocabulary

1. **North-star proposition:** outcome, customer, and boundary in one read.
2. **Scope map:** in, out, and interface with adjacent teams; use a real
   boundary or relationship diagram.
3. **Milestone path:** ordered phases with dates, owners, and exit evidence.
4. **Working model:** decision rights, cadence, and dependencies shown as a
   linked structure rather than equal panels.
5. **Risk and ask:** the few risks that change the plan plus the decision
   needed now.

Use native connectors, tables, and timelines. Use a contextual image only for
identity or a human opening. Keep diagrams sparse enough to trace but dense
enough to explain a dependency. Never add arrows or nodes without a named
relationship.

## Layers and review

If a full-bleed image is used, place a solid or directional scrim behind
editable copy; check that dates, connectors, and source notes remain visible.
Avoid card walls, random geometry, neon gradients, and decorative checkmarks.
The bundled PPJ/PPTX is a source-bound migration of the historical Codex
reference. It can be inspected for native continuation, but every new kickoff
must be recomposed from the project's actual outcome and owners. Check, build,
render, and re-import before delivery.
