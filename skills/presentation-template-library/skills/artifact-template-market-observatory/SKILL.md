# Market Observatory

## Communication territory

An analytical observatory that combines maps, timelines, and comparative evidence without becoming a dashboard.

Use this style for industry landscape report, market structure analysis, competitive intelligence. Avoid it for minimal ceremonial keynote, playful internal culture.

This is a visual direction, not a page library. Read the calibration images for hierarchy, rhythm, and relationships. Build every slide from the current content and do not trace sample geometry or reuse sample wording.

## Design grammar

- **Composition:** Let one landscape visual occupy most of the page and place interpretation on its edge, not in separate cards.
- **Typography:** Humanist sans-serif headlines pair with narrow labels and compact source notes.
- **Geometry and surface:** Use concentric fields, horizon lines, calibrated ticks, and category bands with mostly square corners.
- **Evidence:** Charts should reveal position, movement, or concentration; use direct labels and visible time windows.
- **Imagery:** Aerial, infrastructure, or product-detail imagery works when it locates the market in the real world.
- **Recurring motif:** A small horizon marker may repeat as a locator across sections.
- **Density:** Use a dense default, then vary it intentionally between orientation, proof, and decision pages.
- **Palette roles:** Treat #F7F3EA as the base field, #16252D as primary ink, #D96B3B as the decisive accent, #4F7C78 as supporting evidence, and #C8C0B1 as quiet structure. Adapt values when a user design system is authoritative.

## Page behavior

- Give every page one audience task, one claim, and one primary visual carrier.
- Derive new page silhouettes from this grammar. Do not repeat one arrangement across the deck.
- Keep charts, diagrams, images, and typography in one information relationship; do not place them as independent decorations.
- Use native editable PowerPoint objects and licensed or user-provided imagery. Render and review every page.

## Avoid

- Logo graveyards.
- Equal competitor boxes.
- Rainbow categories.
- Do not mix another template's visual language. A user-provided design system or reference deck remains authoritative.
- Do not copy the calibration text or coordinates; they demonstrate behavior, not reusable pages.

## Calibration examples

- **Cover:** establishes the voice, scale contrast, and opening visual contract.
- **Analysis:** shows how a claim and its supporting relationship share the canvas.
- **Data:** shows how evidence receives visual priority and direct annotation.
- **Closing:** shows how the direction compresses into a specific decision or next move.
