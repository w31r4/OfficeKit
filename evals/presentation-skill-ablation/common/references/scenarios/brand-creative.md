# Scenario: brand and creative

## Audience task

Help an audience remember a distinct promise, feel its relevance, and choose to
engage without sacrificing comprehension or native editability. Lock the
promise, proof, desired feeling, invitation, brand authority, and usage rights
for every asset.

A memorable deck still needs a communication job. Visual novelty cannot replace
proof or a specific next action.

## Narrative

Create tension, curiosity, or desire; reveal the promise; show the product or
idea in use; prove the claim; expand the world; and end with a specific
invitation. Use few words when image, scale, crop, sequence, or typography can
carry the meaning. Break the pattern when a proof page needs analytical clarity.

## Density and rhythm

Build strong contrast between reveal, detail, proof, and invitation. Sparse
pages are valid when the dominant visual earns the space. Follow an emotional
beat with concrete proof. Vary crop, scale, negative space, and visual tempo
without changing the deck's identity on every page.

## Relationship-to-carrier choices

- image-led opening with one memorable line;
- product, idea, or identity reveal;
- detail crop, material study, or transformation sequence;
- proof through demonstration, quote, or measured result;
- type-led statement or native-vector composition;
- world-building sequence using related assets;
- closing invitation with one clear action.

Images and SVG should be intentional assets, not placeholders. Use native
vectors and typography when they better express identity. Charts and tables
may enter proof pages, but should inherit the deck's type, line, and color roles
without hiding the evidence.

## Visual grammar

Choose one distinctive motif, a deliberate crop language, expressive scale,
and a controlled type system. Define background, image, type, line, and accent
roles for this deck. Use asymmetry, overlap, or large negative space only when
they strengthen the promise and preserve reading order.

Repetition should build recognition, not substitute for invention. Keep
geometry, edge treatment, and micro-decoration consistent across images, SVG,
and native objects. Motion may reveal or connect meaning; it cannot compensate
for generic static pages.

## Avoid

Stock-image filler, slogan stacks, gratuitous gradients, random icon clouds,
every page using the same hero crop, decorative circles without a role,
unlicensed assets, and novelty unrelated to the promise.

## Review questions

- Is the promise distinct, memorable, and supported by something concrete?
- Does the visual system feel authored for this idea rather than selected from
  a component library?
- Are image, typography, native objects, and motion carrying one meaning?
- Is the invitation specific and natural?
- If the deck feels generic, should the asset, crop, composition, or promise be
  repaired before adding more effects?
