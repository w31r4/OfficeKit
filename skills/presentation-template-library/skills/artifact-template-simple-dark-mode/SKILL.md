# Simple Dark Mode

## Role and communication job

Use this style when the brief matches **dark presentation, high-contrast visual deck, product narrative, and stage presentation**. It is designed for
general, internal, and external audiences. A quiet stage: one idea owns the frame, with contrast and pacing doing the work that decoration usually tries to do. The deck should help the audience make the
change implied by the work, not merely browse attractive pages. Its natural
story arc is **hero claim → explanation → proof → implication → call to action**. It is not a good fit for **print-first handout or dense operating scorecard**; choose
another style or record the mismatch instead of forcing this grammar.

## Visual grammar

### Surface, palette, and type

- Use a dark, nearly neutral surface and one deliberate light or chromatic accent. Preserve enough contrast for a reader, not only a room.
- Use a large claim, few supporting words, small but present evidence notes, and intentional line breaks. The body remains readable at distance and in export.
- Keep units, dates, source basis, and uncertainty adjacent to the evidence
  they qualify. A visual hierarchy must not erase the conditions behind a claim.

### Geometry, layers, and motif

- Create a clear focal axis with an image, large number, or single diagram. Use scale and crop rather than many floating surfaces.
- Use native Office text, charts, tables, connectors, SVG, and images when they
  carry the page's claim. Keep important foreground content editable.
- For a full-page image, use the real scene stack: background image → tested
  scrim or contrast field → editable claim/evidence → foreground markers. Keep
  axes, labels, connectors, decision lines, and chart marks unobstructed. If the
  stack cannot be proven after rendering, use a bounded image region or refuse
  the treatment.

## Page composition

### Preferred page archetypes

Use these archetypes as a vocabulary, not fixed layouts:

1. **hero proposition** — define the audience task, scope, and one visual
   signal before adding supporting material.
2. **visual explanation** — give one dominant carrier the space needed to be
   understood, with the shortest useful explanation.
3. **proof point** — expose the relationship, method, or comparison with
   lines, axes, labels, or a measured table.
4. **call to action** — state the implication, owner, condition, or next
   move; do not end on an ornamental summary.

### Information carriers

- Favor a hero image, large metric, one relationship diagram, or a single chart with direct labels. A sparse page must still reveal the evidence behind its claim.
- Prefer a strong user or source image cropped as a field. Use an overlay when text crosses it and disclose illustrative imagery when needed.
- A chart, table, photograph, SVG, or geometric composition is valid only when
  it communicates scale, sequence, comparison, context, or identity. Remove
  decorative shapes that do not support the page's conclusion.
- Prefer direct labels and short reading cues over unexplained legends or icons.
  If a framework has no meaningful relationship to show, use typography and a
  rule instead of manufacturing a diagram.

## Density and rhythm

The expected density is **sparse by design: concentrate words and objects around one focal axis, then use a few quiet slides to give the audience breathing room.**. Alternate stillness and reveal. A chapter or hero may be quiet, while a proof page earns density through one legible carrier. Do not distribute
whitespace evenly by habit; concentrate it around the title-to-body transition,
carrier edges, column gutters, and final implication. If the brief calls for a
sparse page, record what the empty field is doing.

## Style-specific playbook

- **Page skeleton.** Set the claim in a strong field, give it one visual proof, and let the next page deepen or resolve the implication.
- **Type rhythm.** Keep the title, evidence, caption, unit, and source roles
  distinct. Let the largest type carry the reading decision, not the ornament.
- **Evidence carriers.** Favor a hero image, large metric, one relationship diagram, or a single chart with direct labels. A sparse page must still reveal the evidence behind its claim.
- **Images and layers.** Prefer a strong user or source image cropped as a field. Use an overlay when text crosses it and disclose illustrative imagery when needed. Inspect crop, contrast, and z-order after
  rendering; do not call an obscured line or label intentional without evidence.
- **Pacing.** Alternate stillness and reveal. A chapter or hero may be quiet, while a proof page earns density through one legible carrier. Let adjacent pages change energy or silhouette when
  the narrative changes, while the design grammar remains recognizable.
- **Selection rule.** Choose this style when contrast, pacing, and a single visual idea matter more than exhaustive tabular detail.

## Signature moves and variation limits

- Repeat a focal axis and accent role, not a black panel recipe.
- Use contrast to mark the active idea; keep secondary evidence quiet but available.
- Let a full-bleed image support the claim without becoming the claim by itself.
- If a user supplies a brand system or reference authority, it wins. This guide
  fills undefined choices and must not be blended with a conflicting style.

## Anti-patterns and refusal boundaries

- Avoid black slides covered in floating pills, random glow, decorative rings, and tiny body type hidden under the hero.
- Do not use darkness to conceal missing evidence or poor contrast.
- Do not put a foreground label over busy image detail without a tested contrast field.
- Do not invent facts, sources, or visual meaning. Mark unresolved design facts
  as unresolved and ask only when the conflict changes the communication result.
- If a requested effect would hide evidence, alter an unknown native object, or
  require a fixed page clone, keep the safe portions and state the boundary.

## Calibration evidence

Inspect all four example images before composing. The four examples demonstrate hero, explanation, proof, and action roles; they calibrate contrast and pacing while leaving composition free. Recompose
every real page from its communication task. Never reuse their wording,
geometry, image bytes, or implied source deck.

## Review checklist

- The page has one audience task, one conclusion, and one primary visual carrier.
- Check title read-through, source/basis treatment, units, labels, and whether
  the final implication follows from the evidence.
- Text, image crop, contrast field, chart marks, connectors, and foreground
  labels survive a rendered visual check without occlusion or overflow.
- The deck varies page silhouette and density with purpose instead of repeating
  a wall of equal modules.
- Reopen the exported PPTX and structurally inspect it. Record the absolute
  path, SHA-256, and visual-review state before delivery.
