# Scenario: analysis and decision

## Audience task

Help an informed audience compare evidence, understand tradeoffs, and make a
specific decision. The deck should reduce uncertainty rather than manufacture
confidence.

## Narrative

Frame the decision, establish criteria, show the evidence and alternatives,
surface uncertainty, recommend a path, then make the next decision explicit.
Use claim-led titles. Put the decisive evidence before the recommendation.

## Density and rhythm

Alternate compact decision frames with denser evidence pages. Preserve common
scales across comparable charts. Give exceptions and uncertainty enough space
to be read, not relegated to tiny footnotes.

## Visual carriers and archetypes

- annotated trend or variance chart;
- option comparison on shared criteria;
- driver tree or causal chain;
- evidence table with one highlighted decision row;
- recommendation page with conditions and triggers;
- scenario range rather than a single false-precision forecast.

## Visual grammar

Use restrained surfaces, straight geometry, explicit axes, thin dividers, and
one high-contrast decision color. Prefer direct labels and aligned baselines.
Use boxes only when they encode comparable alternatives or bounded states.
Connectors must carry direction or causality.

## Avoid

Dashboard walls, ornamental KPI cards, incomparable scales, decorative arrows,
equal emphasis on every metric, recommendations before evidence, and confidence
language unsupported by the source.

## Review questions

- Can the audience state the decision and criteria after scanning the deck?
- Does each visual change the decision, or merely decorate a number?
- Are uncertainty and counter-evidence visible before the conclusion?
- Is the requested action explicit, bounded, and supported?
