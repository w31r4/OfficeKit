# Business Review

## Role and communication job

Use this style when the brief matches **quarterly business review, executive performance decision, and board operating outlook**. It is designed for
executives, board members, and business leaders. A measured executive brief: evidence leads, decisions land visibly, and page rhythm moves from signal to implication to action. The deck should help the audience make the
change implied by the work, not merely browse attractive pages. Its natural
story arc is **stance → performance signal → drivers → implication → decision and next action**. It is not a good fit for **playful consumer launch or image-led cultural story**; choose
another style or record the mismatch instead of forcing this grammar.

## Visual grammar

### Surface, palette, and type

- Use an open paper surface, one ink color, and one restrained accent. The accent marks the decision, current period, or risk; it does not decorate every metric.
- Use a compact overline, sentence-case conclusion title, readable body, and quiet source/date role. The conclusion should be larger than its decoration; never shrink body type to rescue an overfilled page.
- Keep units, dates, source basis, and uncertainty adjacent to the evidence
  they qualify. A visual hierarchy must not erase the conditions behind a claim.

### Geometry, layers, and motif

- Use shared left axes, long rules, annotated marks, and one asymmetric evidence field. Change the silhouette between statement, trend, comparison, and decision pages rather than repeating panels.
- Use native Office text, charts, tables, connectors, SVG, and images when they
  carry the page's claim. Keep important foreground content editable.
- For a full-page image, use the real scene stack: background image → tested
  scrim or contrast field → editable claim/evidence → foreground markers. Keep
  axes, labels, connectors, decision lines, and chart marks unobstructed. If the
  stack cannot be proven after rendering, use a bounded image region or refuse
  the treatment.

## Page composition

### Preferred page archetypes

Use these archetypes as a vocabulary, not fixed layouts:

1. **executive proposition** — define the audience task, scope, and one visual
   signal before adding supporting material.
2. **performance trend** — give one dominant carrier the space needed to be
   understood, with the shortest useful explanation.
3. **driver diagnosis** — expose the relationship, method, or comparison with
   lines, axes, labels, or a measured table.
4. **decision and next move** — state the implication, owner, condition, or next
   move; do not end on an ornamental summary.

### Information carriers

- Favor one dominant chart or comparison per page, direct labels, baselines, annotated marks, and a narrow read-through only when it adds a decision.
- Use documentary or product imagery only when it explains context. A full-page image may sit behind editable text only after crop, contrast, and layer order are checked.
- A chart, table, photograph, SVG, or geometric composition is valid only when
  it communicates scale, sequence, comparison, context, or identity. Remove
  decorative shapes that do not support the page's conclusion.
- Prefer direct labels and short reading cues over unexplained legends or icons.
  If a framework has no meaningful relationship to show, use typography and a
  rule instead of manufacturing a diagram.

## Density and rhythm

The expected density is **medium: alternate a quiet proposition with dense evidence, preserving enough basis for an executive to challenge the conclusion.**. Move from signal to implication to action. Reserve a visual pause for the stance, then earn density with real evidence. Do not distribute
whitespace evenly by habit; concentrate it around the title-to-body transition,
carrier edges, column gutters, and final implication. If the brief calls for a
sparse page, record what the empty field is doing.

## Style-specific playbook

- **Page skeleton.** Start with an assertion and a basis line. Let the body switch between a wide chart, an asymmetric comparison, and a concise implication rail; finish with the decision or open question.
- **Type rhythm.** Keep the title, evidence, caption, unit, and source roles
  distinct. Let the largest type carry the reading decision, not the ornament.
- **Evidence carriers.** Favor one dominant chart or comparison per page, direct labels, baselines, annotated marks, and a narrow read-through only when it adds a decision.
- **Images and layers.** Use documentary or product imagery only when it explains context. A full-page image may sit behind editable text only after crop, contrast, and layer order are checked. Inspect crop, contrast, and z-order after
  rendering; do not call an obscured line or label intentional without evidence.
- **Pacing.** Move from signal to implication to action. Reserve a visual pause for the stance, then earn density with real evidence. Let adjacent pages change energy or silhouette when
  the narrative changes, while the design grammar remains recognizable.
- **Selection rule.** Choose this style when the audience must make or align around an operating or investment decision from comparable evidence.

## Signature moves and variation limits

- Repeat the title-to-evidence axis and source treatment, but let each page choose its own contour.
- Use one accent for the active decision, current period, or exception; keep historical context neutral.
- A read-through line is a conclusion, not a decorative footer.
- If a user supplies a brand system or reference authority, it wins. This guide
  fills undefined choices and must not be blended with a conflicting style.

## Anti-patterns and refusal boundaries

- Do not use dashboard mosaics, equal card grids, decorative rings, or generic metric boxes.
- Do not leave a page without a decision, implication, or explicit unresolved question.
- Do not place a chart label, connector, or decision line behind an image or scrim.
- Do not invent facts, sources, or visual meaning. Mark unresolved design facts
  as unresolved and ask only when the conflict changes the communication result.
- If a requested effect would hide evidence, alter an unknown native object, or
  require a fixed page clone, keep the safe portions and state the boundary.

## Calibration evidence

Inspect all four example images before composing. The four examples demonstrate an opening proposition, evidence diagnosis, data comparison, and decision close; they are fictional calibration pages, not source pages. Recompose
every real page from its communication task. Never reuse their wording,
geometry, image bytes, or implied source deck.

## Review checklist

- The page has one audience task, one conclusion, and one primary visual carrier.
- Check title read-through, source/basis treatment, units, labels, and whether
  the final implication follows from the evidence.
- Text, image crop, contrast field, chart marks, connectors, and foreground
  labels survive a rendered visual check without occlusion or overflow.
- The deck varies page silhouette and density with purpose instead of repeating
  a wall of equal modules.
- Reopen the exported PPTX and structurally inspect it. Record the absolute
  path, SHA-256, and visual-review state before delivery.
