# Team Alignment

## Communication territory

Use this direction for an onsite, planning session, or operating-model reset
where a group must leave with shared context, priorities, and explicit next
steps. It can carry a human opening and dense decision evidence in the same
deck. It is not a generic culture poster.

## Visual grammar

Start with a generous light field and a deep blue or teal display color. A
wide photographic band can establish place or people; interior pages return to
clear type, thin rules, and one accent for the chosen priority. Use a calm
sans-serif hierarchy and let the baseline, not a set of panels, align the
reading path. Warm neutrals may support a photo, but do not mix a rainbow of
status colors.

## Page vocabulary

1. **Place and purpose:** where the team is, why it is meeting, and the outcome
   expected by the end.
2. **Shared context:** a timeline, customer journey, or operating fact that
   gives the group a common starting point.
3. **Priority field:** three or fewer priorities on a shared axis, each with a
   measure and owner.
4. **Working agreement:** cadence, decision rights, and escalation path shown
   as a real relationship.
5. **Commitment close:** actions, owners, and dates visible in one ledger.

Use photography when it gives the group identity, diagrams when relationships
matter, and tables when commitments must be audited. Alternate human pauses
with evidence pages; do not repeat a photo crop or turn every priority into a
rounded card.

## Layers and review

For a photo background, use the scene order image, contrast field, editable
copy, and foreground labels; test the crop and check all text at presentation
size. Avoid stock-photo wallpaper, floating icons, random circles, and card
walls. The bundled PPJ/PPTX is a source-bound migration of the historical
Codex reference and may be continued only when the user asks. Recompose from
the actual team context, then check, render, and re-import the result.
