# Simple Light Mode

## Communication territory

Use this neutral direction when the content needs a quiet, readable surface:
briefings, research notes, handouts, and evidence that may be read without a
speaker. It is intentionally flexible, but not empty. Choose another style
when a brand, photo-led campaign, or dark stage identity is authoritative.

## Visual grammar

Use a white or very light warm field, near-black type, and one cool accent for
links, selected data, or a section cue. Let typography, baselines, and short
rules create hierarchy before adding a surface. Keep a clear display role,
body role, data role, and source role. Titles are left-aligned conclusions;
units, dates, and caveats sit next to the evidence they qualify.

## Page vocabulary

- **Quiet opening:** title, scope, and one sentence of purpose.
- **Evidence page:** one chart, table, diagram, or image with the explanation
  needed to read it.
- **Comparison page:** a shared baseline and two or three explicit contrasts.
- **Method or process:** numbered steps with real relationships and no filler
  arrows.
- **Decision page:** implication, condition, owner, and next action.

Change density across the deck. A light surface does not require a large empty
middle; evidence pages should use their canvas while keeping readable gutters.
Native charts, tables, and lines carry the meaning. A photo can be a context
or section image, never a generic texture.

## Layers and review

For an image-led page, use a native image behind a tested contrast field and
editable text. Never let a line, label, or chart mark disappear into white or
behind an image. Avoid repeated cards, pills, heavy shadows, and arbitrary
colored blocks. The reference PPJ/PPTX is a source-bound migration of the
historical Codex light deck; treat it as inspectable evidence, not as a fixed
layout. Render at reading size and re-import before delivery.
