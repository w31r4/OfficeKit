# Material Evidence

## Communication territory

A tactile evidence style that treats surfaces, process details, and provenance as primary information.

Use this style for craft process story, material innovation, sustainability narrative. Avoid it for abstract cloud architecture, high frequency market report.

This is a visual direction, not a page library. Read the calibration images for hierarchy, rhythm, and relationships. Build every slide from the current content and do not trace sample geometry or reuse sample wording.

## Design grammar

- **Composition:** Let one material image dominate, then integrate measurements, process steps, or impact data into its edge.
- **Typography:** Warm serif headlines pair with utilitarian labels and precise units.
- **Geometry and surface:** Use natural tonal fields, specimen outlines, cutaway diagrams, and scale markers.
- **Evidence:** Impact charts must show the relevant unit and comparison basis; avoid decorative eco metrics.
- **Imagery:** Use macro photography, process documentation, and diagrams with honest color and visible texture.
- **Recurring motif:** A specimen number and thin contour line can recur throughout the deck.
- **Density:** Use a medium default, then vary it intentionally between orientation, proof, and decision pages.
- **Palette roles:** Treat #E9E2D4 as the base field, #27231E as primary ink, #8C5E3C as the decisive accent, #486B58 as supporting evidence, and #B8AA95 as quiet structure. Adapt values when a user design system is authoritative.

## Page behavior

- Give every page one audience task, one claim, and one primary visual carrier.
- Derive new page silhouettes from this grammar. Do not repeat one arrangement across the deck.
- Keep charts, diagrams, images, and typography in one information relationship; do not place them as independent decorations.
- Use native editable PowerPoint objects and licensed or user-provided imagery. Render and review every page.

## Avoid

- Greenwashing motifs.
- Fake paper textures.
- Unscaled material images.
- Do not mix another template's visual language. A user-provided design system or reference deck remains authoritative.
- Do not copy the calibration text or coordinates; they demonstrate behavior, not reusable pages.

## Calibration examples

- **Cover:** establishes the voice, scale contrast, and opening visual contract.
- **Analysis:** shows how a claim and its supporting relationship share the canvas.
- **Data:** shows how evidence receives visual priority and direct annotation.
- **Closing:** shows how the direction compresses into a specific decision or next move.
