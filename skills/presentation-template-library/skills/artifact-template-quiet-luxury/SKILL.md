# Quiet Luxury

## Communication territory

A restrained premium language in which material quality, proportion, and pacing create authority.

Use this style for premium brand presentation, heritage product story, architecture portfolio. Avoid it for incident response, dense operational review.

This is a visual direction, not a page library. Read the calibration images for hierarchy, rhythm, and relationships. Build every slide from the current content and do not trace sample geometry or reuse sample wording.

## Design grammar

- **Composition:** Use one exceptional image, object, or line of type as the page's center; every secondary element must earn its presence.
- **Typography:** Refined serif display text and quiet sans-serif captions create contrast without decorative typography.
- **Geometry and surface:** Use generous fields, precise hairlines, shallow tonal contrast, and carefully aligned product details.
- **Evidence:** Charts should be rare, monochrome, and integrated with the narrative rather than treated as a separate visual language.
- **Imagery:** Use high-resolution detail photography with deliberate lighting and crop; never fill space with lifestyle stock.
- **Recurring motif:** A fine brass rule may recur at major transitions only.
- **Density:** Use a sparse default, then vary it intentionally between orientation, proof, and decision pages.
- **Palette roles:** Treat #F5F1E9 as the base field, #1E1B18 as primary ink, #8A744B as the decisive accent, #6A7568 as supporting evidence, and #C8BFAF as quiet structure. Adapt values when a user design system is authoritative.

## Page behavior

- Give every page one audience task, one claim, and one primary visual carrier.
- Derive new page silhouettes from this grammar. Do not repeat one arrangement across the deck.
- Keep charts, diagrams, images, and typography in one information relationship; do not place them as independent decorations.
- Use native editable PowerPoint objects and licensed or user-provided imagery. Render and review every page.

## Avoid

- Empty pages without focus.
- Gold gradients.
- Luxury clichés.
- Do not mix another template's visual language. A user-provided design system or reference deck remains authoritative.
- Do not copy the calibration text or coordinates; they demonstrate behavior, not reusable pages.

## Calibration examples

- **Cover:** establishes the voice, scale contrast, and opening visual contract.
- **Analysis:** shows how a claim and its supporting relationship share the canvas.
- **Data:** shows how evidence receives visual priority and direct annotation.
- **Closing:** shows how the direction compresses into a specific decision or next move.
