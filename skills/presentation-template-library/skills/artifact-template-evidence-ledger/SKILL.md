# Evidence Ledger

Use Evidence Ledger for experimental reports, pilot reviews, reliability studies, policy tests, and other decisions where the audience must inspect the claim, method, measurement window, uncertainty, and action rule. It is a poor fit for lifestyle storytelling, cinematic launches, playful consumer campaigns, or decks whose primary job is emotional spectacle.

## Communication stance

Begin with a falsifiable claim and end with a reversible decision. Reveal the outcome only after the assignment logic and measurement rules are visible. Every conclusion should point to a measure, threshold, interval, or source note that the audience can audit. When evidence is illustrative or incomplete, say so in the page footer rather than implying operational authority.

## Palette and surfaces

Use a warm paper field as the default reading surface, deep navy for durable structure and primary text, teal for measured signals, and ochre for thresholds or decisions. Pale blue-gray may distinguish a protocol or evidence band. Reserve the dark navy surface for one decisive handoff, chapter boundary, or closing page; do not alternate dark and light pages casually. Color has a role: teal means observed or selected, ochre means decision or boundary, and neutral gray means comparison or context.

## Typography

Pair a restrained serif display face with a neutral sans-serif operating face. The serif voice carries deck titles, central estimates, and major closing statements. The sans-serif voice carries conclusion headlines, protocols, chart labels, tables, sources, and decision rules. Use conclusion-style headlines. Maintain a clear scale rhythm from headline to body to source; sources may be compact but must remain readable after export. Avoid decorative display fonts, all-caps paragraphs, and long centered copy.

## Geometry, lines, and space

Build order with baselines, rules, axes, numbered gates, and measured spacing. Do not use card walls, pill labels, random circles, or rounded containers for ordinary hierarchy. A single filled field may hold a protocol or selected state when the field has semantic meaning. Connectors represent actual causality or method choice and never cross required labels. Keep line marks, data labels, confidence intervals, and decision thresholds unobscured. Negative space should separate phases of reasoning, not merely leave the page empty.

## Page rhythm

Vary density by responsibility:

- opening pages are moderately sparse and state the claim boundary;
- method pages are dense, procedural, and line-led;
- evidence pages give one plot or table clear dominance;
- uncertainty pages isolate the interval and zero or threshold marker;
- decision pages compress the result into gates, stop rules, and a bounded action;
- the final handoff may use a dark field to distinguish record from analysis.

Do not repeat one layout. Preserve the same type scale, line weight, source position, and semantic color roles while changing the page silhouette.

## Charts, tables, and diagrams

Prefer native editable charts and tables. Use columns for discrete period values, lines for continuous or indexed movement, and a hand-built interval only when the uncertainty geometry must be explained. If a combined bar-line view would cause marks or labels to collide, separate it into adjacent evidence pages rather than distorting either series. Show direct labels only when they remain clear; otherwise use a concise legend or endpoint annotation. Tables use light rules and spacious rows, with treatment differences expressed by text or a single signal color rather than zebra-striping every cell.

Decision trees should contain only the branches required by the actual method. Put YES and NO labels beside their connector segments as independent text objects so they remain readable and editable. Timelines encode phase duration through width and discontinuity marks. Confidence intervals must show the estimate, both bounds, and the no-effect or decision threshold.

## Motion

For hybrid or live delivery, use short fade transitions and animate only evidence whose sequence matters. A chart may reveal by category along time. A method chain may reveal one branch at a time. Reader delivery stays static. Never use motion to compensate for an incomplete composition, and do not auto-advance.

## Variation limits and anti-patterns

Keep the warm-paper, navy, teal, and ochre semantic system intact. You may adjust exact values for contrast, but do not introduce a rainbow palette. Keep rules thin and geometric marks purposeful. Avoid dashboards, generic metrics cards, decorative arrows, floating badges, glass effects, gradients without data meaning, and stock photos added only to fill space. Never place a translucent panel over a chart if it hides marks or values.

## What the examples demonstrate

- The cover defines the claim, evidence boundary, and measured profile without a card grid.
- The method tree uses sparse connectors, explicit branch labels, and one selected state.
- The experiment matrix shows how a dense table can remain editorial and readable.
- The incident-hours page gives the native chart dominance and moves interpretation to a narrow read-through column.
- The confidence-interval page makes uncertainty, not decoration, the visual center.
- The gate page turns evidence into an ordered decision and a visible stop rule.
