# Operating Review

## Role and communication job

Use this style when the brief matches **weekly operating review, monthly operating review, and operations scorecard**. It is designed for
executives, operations teams, and functional leadership. A working control surface: the reader can see the cadence, exception, owner, and next move without decorative ceremony. The deck should help the audience make the
change implied by the work, not merely browse attractive pages. Its natural
story arc is **cadence → score → exception → cause → owner action**. It is not a good fit for **external sales pitch or consumer campaign**; choose
another style or record the mismatch instead of forcing this grammar.

## Visual grammar

### Surface, palette, and type

- Keep surfaces quiet and use one alert accent for exceptions. State colors are semantic and should be accompanied by labels, time windows, or thresholds.
- Use terse conclusion titles, compact labels, visible source/date lines, and a readable data role. Do not shrink type to fit a status wall.
- Keep units, dates, source basis, and uncertainty adjacent to the evidence
  they qualify. A visual hierarchy must not erase the conditions behind a claim.

### Geometry, layers, and motif

- Use thresholds, baselines, exception markers, routes, and aligned rows. Let lines and position carry structure before containers do.
- Use native Office text, charts, tables, connectors, SVG, and images when they
  carry the page's claim. Keep important foreground content editable.
- For a full-page image, use the real scene stack: background image → tested
  scrim or contrast field → editable claim/evidence → foreground markers. Keep
  axes, labels, connectors, decision lines, and chart marks unobstructed. If the
  stack cannot be proven after rendering, use a bounded image region or refuse
  the treatment.

## Page composition

### Preferred page archetypes

Use these archetypes as a vocabulary, not fixed layouts:

1. **cadence opener** — define the audience task, scope, and one visual
   signal before adding supporting material.
2. **scorecard with context** — give one dominant carrier the space needed to be
   understood, with the shortest useful explanation.
3. **exception path** — expose the relationship, method, or comparison with
   lines, axes, labels, or a measured table.
4. **owner action close** — state the implication, owner, condition, or next
   move; do not end on an ornamental summary.

### Information carriers

- Use trend charts, threshold lines, small tables, root-cause paths, and explicit owner/date rows. A table is valid when fields are comparable and the next action is visible.
- Use imagery rarely; a process photo or system screenshot must explain an operating constraint and carry a caption.
- A chart, table, photograph, SVG, or geometric composition is valid only when
  it communicates scale, sequence, comparison, context, or identity. Remove
  decorative shapes that do not support the page's conclusion.
- Prefer direct labels and short reading cues over unexplained legends or icons.
  If a framework has no meaningful relationship to show, use typography and a
  rule instead of manufacturing a diagram.

## Density and rhythm

The expected density is **dense: every page answers one operational question, but local whitespace protects labels, units, and the next action.**. Use a steady cadence for routine pages and a deliberate pause for a material exception. Do not make every period visually urgent. Do not distribute
whitespace evenly by habit; concentrate it around the title-to-body transition,
carrier edges, column gutters, and final implication. If the brief calls for a
sparse page, record what the empty field is doing.

## Style-specific playbook

- **Page skeleton.** Lead with a score or trend, route attention through the exception and cause, then land on owner, date, or action.
- **Type rhythm.** Keep the title, evidence, caption, unit, and source roles
  distinct. Let the largest type carry the reading decision, not the ornament.
- **Evidence carriers.** Use trend charts, threshold lines, small tables, root-cause paths, and explicit owner/date rows. A table is valid when fields are comparable and the next action is visible.
- **Images and layers.** Use imagery rarely; a process photo or system screenshot must explain an operating constraint and carry a caption. Inspect crop, contrast, and z-order after
  rendering; do not call an obscured line or label intentional without evidence.
- **Pacing.** Use a steady cadence for routine pages and a deliberate pause for a material exception. Do not make every period visually urgent. Let adjacent pages change energy or silhouette when
  the narrative changes, while the design grammar remains recognizable.
- **Selection rule.** Choose this style when the deck is used to run a recurring operating conversation, not to sell an aspiration.

## Signature moves and variation limits

- Repeat the cadence cue, period, and source placement so the deck can be scanned week to week.
- Use the alert accent only where a threshold or decision changes.
- Keep action text next to its owner and due date.
- If a user supplies a brand system or reference authority, it wins. This guide
  fills undefined choices and must not be blended with a conflicting style.

## Anti-patterns and refusal boundaries

- Avoid decorative dashboards, repeated rounded cards, unlabeled status colors, and dense text walls.
- Do not show an exception without definition, time window, and owner.
- Do not bury a threshold, label, or connector under a photo or fill.
- Do not invent facts, sources, or visual meaning. Mark unresolved design facts
  as unresolved and ask only when the conflict changes the communication result.
- If a requested effect would hide evidence, alter an unknown native object, or
  require a fixed page clone, keep the safe portions and state the boundary.

## Calibration evidence

Inspect all four example images before composing. The four examples demonstrate cadence, diagnosis, data, and action pages with different silhouettes but a shared operating reading path. Recompose
every real page from its communication task. Never reuse their wording,
geometry, image bytes, or implied source deck.

## Review checklist

- The page has one audience task, one conclusion, and one primary visual carrier.
- Check title read-through, source/basis treatment, units, labels, and whether
  the final implication follows from the evidence.
- Text, image crop, contrast field, chart marks, connectors, and foreground
  labels survive a rendered visual check without occlusion or overflow.
- The deck varies page silhouette and density with purpose instead of repeating
  a wall of equal modules.
- Reopen the exported PPTX and structurally inspect it. Record the absolute
  path, SHA-256, and visual-review state before delivery.
