# Presentation doctrine

A presentation is four things at once:

1. a communication event intended to change what an audience knows, understands,
   believes, decides, or does;
2. an editable deck that carries the argument before and after the event;
3. a live or self-guided sequence whose pacing changes comprehension;
4. a native artifact that must remain usable in PowerPoint.

Start from the audience change, not from a collection of pages. Record one
`brief.primaryJob`, any secondary jobs, and a concrete `expectedOutcome`.
Choose `live`, `reader`, or `hybrid` from how the material will be delivered and
used afterward.

Use six distinct review layers:

- **factual**: claims and sources remain traceable;
- **communication**: the deck serves the audience and expected outcome;
- **narrative**: pages form a cumulative argument;
- **cognitive**: density, hierarchy, and explanation fit human attention;
- **visual**: composition makes relationships visible and supports the message;
- **native operation**: the PPTX opens, edits, plays, and preserves its source.

OfficeKit provides composable authoring and verification primitives. The Agent
still owns the thesis, factual judgment, and design choices. Deterministic
warnings locate risks; they do not certify truth or beauty.

If the medium is a weak fit, record `mediumFit: "weak"` and a concrete
`mediumFitNote`, then continue with an honest mitigation. Do not force the user
into another medium.
