# Simple Dark Mode

## Communication territory

Use this direction for operational briefings, infrastructure decisions,
control-room narratives, incident reviews, and technical propositions that
benefit from a nocturnal, observability-led atmosphere. It is strongest when
the deck moves from a real operating context to a measurable signal, a response
chain, and a bounded decision.

The design proposition is **dark environmental evidence plus precise signal
language**. Photography supplies place and operational identity. Cyan lines,
timestamps, and restrained amber accents expose the decision logic. The deck
must remain credible without cinematic imagery; the picture never substitutes
for evidence.

Avoid this direction for archival print reports, children or education decks,
warm lifestyle brands, celebratory consumer campaigns, or dense spreadsheet
appendices.

## Surface and color system

Use a near-black hierarchy with a narrow signal palette:

- `#05080C` is the deepest field and closing surface.
- `#0B1118` is the standard content surface.
- `#111A24` is a secondary structural field, not a card color.
- `#F2F5F7` is primary type.
- `#A9B4C0` is explanatory and source text.
- `#31D3E8` is the live signal, route, active datum, or section marker.
- `#F5A81C` is reserved for one threshold, exception, or decision hinge.

Never distribute several bright colors across one page. Cyan represents a
continuous system or measured signal. Amber identifies the single condition
that changes the decision. Do not add generic blue-purple gradients.

Use flat dark fields for most content. A subtle dark-to-transparent gradient is
allowed only when it creates a readable transition between a photograph and
editable text. It must follow the image's light direction and must not appear as
a decorative glow.

## Typography

Use a complete neutral sans-serif throughout. Prefer Aptos, Arial, Inter, or a
metrics-compatible CJK sans-serif.

- opening and decision claims: 34–48 pt, regular or medium weight;
- interior conclusions: 26–36 pt;
- metrics: 36–54 pt only when unit and basis are adjacent;
- body and explanatory copy: 15–22 pt;
- source, credit, timestamp, and folio: 9–12 pt;
- uppercase signal labels: short, moderately tracked, never paragraph length.

Use size, alignment, and spacing before containers. Do not outline type, apply
neon effects, or use monospaced type merely to look technical. Titles state a
conclusion or operating condition rather than a generic topic.

## Image language and layers

Photography is a primary carrier in this direction. Give every image one
declared role: operating environment, system scale, human context, evidence, or
transition. Use distinct images for distinct recurring roles; do not repeat one
hero crop throughout the deck.

Preferred scene stacks are:

```text
native image background
→ one dark contrast field or directional scrim
→ editable claim, signal route, and source
```

or:

```text
selectable half-slide photograph
→ hard boundary
→ editable evidence field
```

Use full-bleed imagery for an opening or scale statement, and a half-slide image
for operational context. A photograph may be cropped aggressively when its
subject, horizon, and credit remain intact. Do not place body text over a busy
image without a tested scrim. Do not use rounded photo cards, thumbnail walls,
or generic stock images that only fill empty space.

Photographs used as context are not evidence of the synthetic calibration
scenario. Label that boundary. Preserve author, license, source, and any visible
credit required by the asset rights.

## Data, diagrams, and signal lines

Use native editable charts, tables, connectors, and text whenever they carry
the claim.

- charts use transparent plot backgrounds, direct labels, and minimal axes;
- a bar sequence may use cyan for normal stages and one amber bar for the
  threshold or selected outcome;
- signal routes use one thin cyan line with at most three named stages;
- tables are border-light operational ledgers, never colored-cell dashboards;
- diagrams expose real chronology, ownership, causality, or dependency;
- every number includes its unit, period, and basis.

Do not let a line, photograph, mask, bar, or label cover another data mark. Do
not use random circles, equal rounded panels, colored side-strip cards, icon
walls, glass effects, or schematic nodes that do not encode a real relation.

## Page archetypes

Treat these as a vocabulary rather than fixed layouts:

1. **Nocturnal opening** — a real place image occupies half or more of the
   frame; a flat dark field carries one proposition and one short signal rule.
2. **System scale** — a true native background provides geographic or network
   context; one route and a few labels explain the measured chain.
3. **Operational split** — a large documentary image establishes the working
   environment while an editable chart or timeline proves the response.
4. **Threshold decision** — imagery recedes; one large claim, one rule, and two
   aligned evidence groups make the decision explicit.
5. **Exception page** — a single amber mark interrupts an otherwise cyan and
   neutral system to reveal the condition that needs action.

Alternate image-led and evidence-led silhouettes. Do not repeat the same
half-image split on every page.

## Density and rhythm

Use mixed density. Openings may be sparse because image and proposition share
the work. Evidence pages should use the available canvas and may contain one
chart, 3–6 direct labels, a concise interpretation, and a source boundary.
Decision pages can return to typography and negative space.

Negative space must focus a claim, separate image from evidence, or establish a
pause before a decision. Empty center regions and underfilled evidence fields
are defects, not sophistication.

## Calibration map

Inspect all four examples before applying the direction:

- the opening shows a hard dark/image split and restrained signal rule;
- the scale page shows a true image background, full-slide scrim, and editable
  route above the background;
- the response page shows a documentary image beside editable native bars;
- the decision page removes imagery and uses typography, one rule, and aligned
  evidence to close the argument.

The examples contain fictional calibration content and separately licensed
reference photography. Recompose every page for the new communication task. Do
not copy wording, coordinates, source images, or synthetic figures.

## Review

Before delivery, verify:

- each image has a declared role, valid rights record, deliberate crop, and
  visible credit where required;
- every image background remains behind the scrim, text, and data layers;
- all labels, bars, connectors, timestamps, sources, and units are unobscured;
- cyan describes the continuous signal and amber marks only one exception or
  decision hinge;
- the deck alternates image-led, evidence-led, and decision silhouettes;
- no page relies on cards, random geometry, decorative glow, or AI rainbow
  color;
- the PPJ checks, builds, renders, and re-imports with stable editable objects.
